What your first lesson can feel like

1. Tell Gillian what worries you

You can mention specific fears, previous lessons, a long break or that you're starting completely from scratch. You don't need an impressive explanation.

2. Get comfortable with the automatic

You'll learn the basics and begin in a setting appropriate to your experience. There is no clutch to worry about, and Gillian has her own dual controls.

3. Practise, ask and repeat

Lessons last 90 minutes, so there is space to practise a skill again rather than rushing through an hour. Questions and mistakes are part of learning.

4. Agree the next step

After the lesson, Gillian can explain what went well, what needs work and whether another lesson makes sense. You are not signing up to a large package.

Questions nervous learners ask

What if I panic or make a mistake?

You can tell Gillian what is happening. Her car has dual controls and she can help you manage a situation safely. You won't be expected to know everything at the start.

Am I too old to learn to drive?

No. People learn at different ages, and Gillian has helped adults return after years away from driving lessons.

Will I be forced to book lots of lessons?

No. The current advertised arrangement is one £80, 90-minute lesson at a time, without a contract or upfront block booking.

Should I choose an automatic if I'm anxious?

An automatic can reduce the number of tasks you're juggling while learning. It won't remove the need for observation and safe decisions, but many learners prefer it because there's no clutch or gear shifting.
